# Star Princess — Ingredient Bounty Rankings (V2, Bar-First)

**Status:** revised review draft. This replaces the earlier Ingredient Bounty ranking; the Cocktail Bounty file is unchanged.

## What changed

- **Bar coverage determines the tier.** We rank by the number of *listed bar locations* with at least one qualifying cocktail. The number of distinct cocktails is shown as a secondary reference and orders entries within a tier; it does not make a two-bar ingredient cheaper than a four-bar ingredient.
- **One canonical objective per ingredient/flavor family.** No separate “melon” versus “melon liqueur,” “ginger” versus “ginger beer,” “vanilla” versus “vanilla vodka,” or similarly nested objectives. Scope is shown in the label and defined in the notes.
- **Only one ingredient per bounty.** No ingredient combinations. Spirit styles and modifiers are allowed, but a brand is not an objective by itself.
- **Poor hidden hunts are excluded.** A one-bar-only ingredient is excluded. For Elusive objectives (2–4 bars), at least 3 distinct named cocktails must qualify; this avoids near-exact-drink hunts. An ingredient supported by only one provisional menu source is excluded.
- **Menu evidence remains imperfect.** Promenade Bars, Lido Bar, The MIX, and Star Bar reuse one *provisional* pool-bar PDF. Their four bar listings are not four independently verified menus, particularly at Star Bar. The `Menu groups` column treats those four as **one shared source** so you can assess the uncertainty.
- **Separate cocktail and ingredient pools.** A cocktail cut from the Drink Bounty deck can still satisfy an Ingredient Bounty when it is in the full logging menu. First ten Good Spirits cocktails are included provisionally; their prices were not verified.

## Proposed scoring by listed bar coverage

| Bars listing qualifying cocktail | Findability tier | Points |
|---:|---|---:|
| 2–4 | Elusive | 100 |
| 5–7 | Hard to Find | 75 |
| 8–10 | Less Common | 50 |
| 11–13 | Easy to Find | 25 |

One-bar ingredients are excluded by default. For the 2–4 bar tier, there must also be 3+ distinct named matching cocktails. **The bar count is always the ranking input; cocktail count is secondary.**

## Pool size and rotation

- **45 unique proposed Ingredient Bounties** after normalization and usability exclusions.
- **42 scheduled draws** over the seven-day cruise: noon / 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 PM every day. The final window closes at 11 PM.
- **3 unseen** if all 42 are drawn without replacement, and no ingredients are removed after approval. This is a thinner buffer than the prior 54-entry draft; it reflects merging and stricter consistency rather than a new requirement for no repeats.
- Each draw selects one still-unused objective uniformly. There are no per-day tier guarantees. The entire pool may change after your spot check.

## Data caveats / spot-check notes

1. **This is menu-based findability, not actual shipwide ingredient inventory.** The menu is an older snapshot; bartenders might be able to make drinks not listed or might lack a listed item. A menu name/description alone does not prove every ingredient in every real-world recipe.
2. **Bar count does not equal independent menu evidence.** The four pool venue mappings reuse the same PDF; the `Menu groups` column shows this limitation. Rows dominated by shared pool listings are worth special scrutiny.
3. **Good Spirits prices are unverified.** Only its first ten named cocktails are included provisionally, per the organizer’s instructions.
4. **Some same-name cocktails differ by venue.** In particular, Passion Lilly at Cascade and Bellini’s has materially different recipes; a match is counted only at the bar where the ingredient is printed.
5. **This list is intentionally strict about duplicate objective language.** Different ingredient types can still coexist in a single cocktail (e.g. Kahlúa and espresso). The rule is to avoid one objective entirely subsuming another, not to prevent two distinct ingredients from appearing in the same drink.
6. **Pool is tight.** With only 3 unused objectives after 42 draws, it is better to review and perhaps add well-supported categories than pad the pool with one-bar needles-in-haystacks. All matches use only the supplied cocktail recipes and known named product aliases, not inferred onboard stock.
